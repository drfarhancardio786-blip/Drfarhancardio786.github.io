Understanding Blood Pressure: What Do the Numbers Mean?

Medical author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan
Last updated: 25 August 2026

Blood pressure describes the force of blood against artery walls. It is written as systolic pressure, the higher number, and diastolic pressure, the lower number.

Key point: One reading is a snapshot. Diagnosis and treatment decisions usually require clinical context and, when appropriate, repeated measurements.

What do the numbers mean?

  • Systolic: pressure when the heart contracts.
  • Diastolic: pressure between heartbeats.

Getting a better reading

  • Rest quietly for about five minutes.
  • Use an appropriately sized upper-arm cuff.
  • Support the arm at heart level and avoid talking.
  • Repeat measurements when clinically appropriate.

When to take a reading seriously

Persistently elevated readings deserve assessment. Around 180/120 mmHg or higher is very high; if accompanied by chest pain, severe breathlessness, weakness, trouble speaking, confusion, fainting or other severe symptoms, seek urgent medical care.
