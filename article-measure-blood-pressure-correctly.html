How to Measure Blood Pressure Correctly

Accurate blood-pressure measurement depends on standardized technique, an appropriate device and the correct cuff size.

Author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan, MBBS, MSCVmed, MD Cardiology, CCT PIMS, MPhil Cardiovascular Research, Member ACC
Published: 24 August 2026

Use an appropriate device

Choose a validated automated upper-arm cuff monitor and use the cuff size recommended for your arm circumference. If unsure about device suitability, discuss it with a clinician or pharmacist.

Prepare before measuring

  • Avoid smoking, caffeine and exercise for about 30 minutes beforehand.
  • Empty your bladder if needed.
  • Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes.
  • Do not talk or use your phone during measurement.

Position yourself correctly

  • Sit with back supported.
  • Keep feet flat and legs uncrossed.
  • Place the cuff on a bare upper arm.
  • Support the arm at approximately heart level.

Take repeated readings

A single reading can vary. A practical approach is to take two readings about one minute apart and record both, following the monitoring schedule recommended by your clinician.

Common mistakes

  • Measuring immediately after activity or caffeine.
  • Talking during the measurement.
  • Using the wrong cuff size.
  • Measuring over clothing.
  • Leaving the arm unsupported.
  • Making decisions from one isolated reading.

When should you seek urgent help?

If a markedly elevated reading is accompanied by chest pain, severe shortness of breath, new weakness, difficulty speaking, severe confusion or other signs of an acute emergency, seek emergency medical care.
