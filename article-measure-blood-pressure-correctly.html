How to Measure Blood Pressure Correctly

Accurate blood-pressure measurement is essential because technique, device choice and cuff size can materially affect the reading. This guide explains a practical standardized approach for home monitoring.

Author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan, MBBS, MSCVmed, MD Cardiology, CCT PIMS, MPhil Cardiovascular Research, Member ACC
Published: 24 August 2026

1. Use an appropriate device

Choose a validated automated upper-arm cuff monitor and use the cuff size recommended for your arm circumference. Wrist and finger devices may be less reliable and should not be assumed to provide interchangeable readings. citeturn0search1turn0search12

2. Prepare before measuring

  • Avoid smoking, caffeine and exercise for about 30 minutes beforehand.
  • Empty your bladder if needed.
  • Sit quietly for at least 5 minutes before measuring.
  • Do not talk, use your phone or watch television during the rest period or measurement.

These steps are part of standardized home blood-pressure measurement guidance. citeturn0search1turn0search12

3. Sit and position the arm correctly

  • Sit in a chair with your back supported.
  • Keep both feet flat on the floor and avoid crossing your legs.
  • Place the cuff on a bare upper arm.
  • Support the arm at approximately heart level.

NICE also recommends a quiet, standardized environment and an appropriately sized cuff. citeturn0search0

4. Take more than one reading

A single reading can vary. A practical home-monitoring approach is to take two readings about one minute apart and record both. Follow the monitoring schedule recommended by your clinician; recent AHA/ACC guidance describes paired morning and evening readings when structured home monitoring is being performed. citeturn0search12turn0search3

Common mistakes

  • Measuring immediately after activity or caffeine.
  • Talking during the measurement.
  • Using the wrong cuff size.
  • Measuring over clothing.
  • Leaving the arm unsupported or far above/below heart level.
  • Making decisions from one isolated reading.

When should you seek urgent help?

A very high reading should be interpreted in context. If a markedly elevated blood pressure reading is accompanied by symptoms such as chest pain, severe shortness of breath, weakness/numbness, difficulty speaking, severe confusion or other signs of an acute emergency, seek emergency medical care. Do not delay emergency assessment to repeatedly check the monitor.
