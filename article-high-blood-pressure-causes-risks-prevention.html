High Blood Pressure: Causes, Risks and Prevention

High blood pressure (hypertension) is a common cardiovascular risk factor. It often causes no symptoms, which is why accurate measurement and appropriate follow-up are important.

Author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan, MBBS, MSCVmed, MD Cardiology, CCT PIMS, MPhil Cardiovascular Research, Member ACC
Published: 24 August 2026

What causes high blood pressure?

Most adults with hypertension have primary hypertension, where multiple genetic, environmental and lifestyle factors contribute. Secondary hypertension can result from specific medical conditions or medicines and may need targeted investigation.

Why does it matter?

Persistently elevated blood pressure can increase long-term risk of cardiovascular disease, stroke, kidney disease and other complications. Risk depends on the overall clinical picture, not only one number.

Important risk factors

  • Increasing age and family history
  • Excess body weight and physical inactivity
  • High dietary sodium intake and unhealthy dietary patterns
  • Excess alcohol and some medicines or substances
  • Chronic kidney disease, sleep-related breathing disorders and selected endocrine conditions

Prevention and risk reduction

Useful measures include regular physical activity, a heart-healthy dietary pattern, maintaining a healthy body weight, avoiding tobacco, limiting excess alcohol and taking prescribed treatment consistently. Individual targets should be discussed with a clinician.

Do not diagnose yourself from one reading

Blood pressure varies. Modern guidelines emphasize standardized measurement and repeated office or out-of-office readings when evaluating suspected hypertension.
