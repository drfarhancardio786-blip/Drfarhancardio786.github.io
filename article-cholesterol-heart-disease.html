Cholesterol and Heart Disease: What Is the Connection?

Medical author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan
Published: 25 August 2026

Cholesterol is essential for normal body functions, but certain cholesterol-containing particles can contribute to the development of atherosclerosis when they accumulate in artery walls over time.

What is atherosclerosis?

Atherosclerosis is a gradual process involving plaque formation within arteries. Over time, plaques can narrow arteries or become unstable and contribute to events such as heart attack or ischemic stroke.

Why LDL matters

LDL cholesterol is a major modifiable contributor to atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease. The importance of a particular LDL result depends on cumulative exposure and an individual's overall risk profile.

Risk factors work together

High blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, kidney disease, family history and other factors can interact with cholesterol-related risk. Prevention is therefore broader than focusing on one laboratory number.

What can you do?

Maintain a heart-healthy lifestyle, avoid tobacco, remain physically active and obtain individualized assessment when risk factors or abnormal results are present. People with established cardiovascular disease often require more intensive risk management.
