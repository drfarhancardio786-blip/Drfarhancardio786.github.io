Heart-Healthy Nutrition: A Practical Guide

Heart-healthy eating is less about a single “perfect” food and more about the overall pattern over time. A useful approach emphasizes vegetables and fruit, fibre-rich foods, appropriate protein sources and predominantly unsaturated fats.

Think pattern, not perfection: Overall diet quality matters more than occasional individual foods.

Build a balanced plate

  • Make vegetables and fruit a substantial part of meals.
  • Choose whole or minimally processed, fibre-rich carbohydrates.
  • Include appropriate protein sources.
  • Prefer unsaturated fats such as those in nuts, seeds, fish and many plant oils.

Watch sodium and highly processed foods

Reducing excessive sodium can help many people manage blood pressure. Highly processed foods may contribute substantial sodium, added sugars and calories.

Portions matter

Even nutritious foods contain calories. Portion size should be considered alongside food quality, activity and individual goals.

When individualized advice matters

People with kidney disease, diabetes, heart failure, pregnancy, food allergies or other specific needs may require individualized nutrition advice.

References
