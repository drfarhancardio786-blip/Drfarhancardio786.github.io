How Insulin Resistance Develops

Medical author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan
Published: 27 August 2026

Insulin helps regulate how the body handles glucose and other nutrients. Insulin resistance means that tissues do not respond normally to insulin, so the body may need to produce more insulin to maintain glucose control.

Key point: Insulin resistance is influenced by genetics, body composition, physical activity, sleep and other metabolic factors. It is not explained by one food or one laboratory value.

What happens over time?

Compensatory insulin production may initially maintain glucose levels. If compensation becomes insufficient, glucose can rise and prediabetes or type 2 diabetes may develop.

Why does it matter for the heart?

Insulin resistance may occur alongside high triglycerides, low HDL cholesterol, high blood pressure and central obesity, contributing to a broader cardiometabolic risk pattern.

What can help?

Regular physical activity, improved dietary quality, weight management when appropriate and treatment of associated conditions can improve overall metabolic health. Individualized medical care is important.
