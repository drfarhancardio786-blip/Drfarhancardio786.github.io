Understanding Cholesterol: LDL, HDL and Cardiovascular Risk

Medical author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan
Last updated: 23 August 2026

Cholesterol is a wax-like substance needed for normal body functions, but the amount and type of cholesterol circulating in the blood can influence long-term cardiovascular risk.

Key point: A lipid result should be interpreted in context. The same LDL cholesterol value may have different implications for people with different overall cardiovascular risk.

What do LDL, HDL and triglycerides mean?

  • LDL cholesterol: often called “bad” cholesterol because higher levels can contribute to atherosclerotic plaque formation.
  • HDL cholesterol: is associated with cholesterol transport, but a high HDL value does not cancel the risk associated with other factors.
  • Triglycerides: are another blood lipid and may be influenced by diet, alcohol, metabolic health and some medical conditions.

Why overall risk matters

Age, blood pressure, diabetes, smoking, kidney disease, family history and established cardiovascular disease can substantially change a person's baseline risk. People with known atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease are generally managed differently from those without established disease.

Can lifestyle help?

A dietary pattern rich in vegetables, fruit, legumes, whole grains and other minimally processed foods can support cardiovascular health. Regular physical activity, avoiding tobacco and achieving an appropriate weight can also help overall risk reduction.

When should you discuss results with a clinician?

Very high cholesterol values, premature cardiovascular disease in the family, diabetes, kidney disease, established cardiovascular disease or uncertainty about treatment are good reasons for individualized assessment.
