HbA1c Explained: What Your Result Means

Medical author: Dr. Farhan Ahmed Khan
Published: 27 August 2026

HbA1c is a laboratory measure related to glucose exposure over time. It is commonly used to help diagnose and monitor diabetes.

Key point: HbA1c is useful, but it is not perfect. Conditions affecting red blood cells or hemoglobin can make interpretation less straightforward.

What does HbA1c reflect?

Because red blood cells circulate for weeks, HbA1c generally provides information about average glycaemic exposure over the preceding months rather than a single moment.

Why can results differ from finger-stick or sensor readings?

Daily glucose readings show immediate values and patterns, whereas HbA1c is a longer-term marker. Both can provide useful but different information.

When is extra interpretation needed?

Anemia, recent blood loss or transfusion, some hemoglobin variants and other conditions can affect reliability. Clinicians may use additional tests when the result does not match the clinical picture.
